# Data attribution

Team rosters in `teams/` were generated by `tools/generate_teams.py` from the
Lahman Baseball Database (Sean Lahman, https://sabr.org/lahman-database/),
used under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike license (CC BY-SA).

What comes from Lahman: batting and pitching lines, fielding ratings (range, errors and arm,
measured against the league average at each position that season), each season's league error and
strikeout rates, and each ballpark's overall scoring factor (the average of Lahman's batting and
pitching park factors).

What does not: the fence distances and wall heights in `stadiums.json` were entered by hand from
memory of the real ballparks and are approximate. Home-run and extra-base factors are derived
from those distances by the formulas in the script, not measured.

Wall colours (`wallColor`, as #rrggbb, used by the animated field) are set only where a source supports them:
Fenway Park (the Green Monster is painted green) and Shea Stadium in 1969 (the Mets' history timeline on MLB.com
says the outfield wall was repainted dark green in May 1967). The shades are chosen for the picture, not matched to
paint. Every other park has no `wallColor` and keeps the field's default wall; add one to a park's entry to change it.

## Organ melodies

The tunes in `js/sound.js` are public-domain compositions, kept as ABC notation so the notes can be checked:

- Take Me Out to the Ball Game (Albert Von Tilzer, 1908): chorus, from Erich Rickheit's traditional-tune ABC collection.
- La Raspa / Mexican Hat Dance (traditional): transcription by John Chambers.
- Hail to the Chief (James Sanderson, 1812): transcription by John Chambers.
- Funeral March (Chopin, Piano Sonata No. 2): the opening bars, read from a MIDI file of the score.
- Charge!: the traditional cavalry call.

The tune-to-moment mapping (seventh-inning stretch, rally, new pitcher, ejection) is in `Services/SoundService.cs`.
